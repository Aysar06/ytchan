## The foundation you can't skip

If you're using AI to generate your content, there's nothing wrong with that. But you still can't skip the foundation: **knowing who it is that you're making it for.**

Ask AI for a script, a title, or a thumbnail without knowing who you're making it for — that's the best combination to get slop. **This is why so much of that content looks and feels the same: because it's not directed toward a specific person.**

Some of you may be thinking, "hold on, I do use AI for strategy." Which is fair, and you're probably a step ahead of the creators that don't. But after working with a ton of creators, one of the things I see is that those questions we're asking are just **surface level.** You can ask "help me find my niche" or "give me content ideas" — but if you're not looking for content ideas **for a specific type of person, dealing with a specific type of issue, looking for a specific type of thing**, those content ideas are just going to be hit or miss, and sometimes just miss.

## The real channel walkthrough

Let me run this on a real channel. I don't have any inside information on this channel — this is all public stuff we can all see. AI just allows you to do it quicker.

This is **Mary Katherine's Table.** Southern cooking, around **103 subscribers**, growing at about **8% a month.** I picked it because **the comments are gold** — it's full of people narrating their lives in there.

I'm going to use the **vidIQ Claude MCP**, but you can also do this with the AI coach or the ChatGPT app.

### Prompt 1 — mine the comments

> *Pull the top comments from this channel's most engaged recent videos. Not the surface-level patterns — what people are repeatedly saying about themselves, their lives, what they're doing while they're watching, and what they want more of. Give me a summary.*

And look at what comes up over and over again:

- "I'm 75 years old." "Southern lady, age 78." A commenter whose handle is literally **Beauty Over 70.**
- They're naming **regions** — North Georgia, East Tennessee, Coastal North Carolina.
- Talking about **shucking corn with his siblings**, and **hunting for antique dishes** like the ones in her shop.
- "It is *dash* for salt."
- One says she **uses lemons because she's watching her sodium.**
- And one line really worth paying attention to: **"I love to watch you in the kitchen, it's so relaxing."**

**That's not just somebody watching a recipe. That's somebody watching for company.**

A lot of times when I ask creators who their audience is, our first instinct is to go to analytics and say "well, YouTube says my analytics are XYZ." But **those analytics aren't fully reliable, because that's just the age of the people who have those accounts.** Here you get that information straight from the people who are already showing up in your content — or showing up in the content of people doing what you want to do.

**And that right there is the whole game. We're not generating, we're not creating anything yet. We're using AI to speed up the research that is necessary for us to understand our audience.**

### Prompt 2 — build the avatar

> *I want you to come up with a detailed profile of your avatar. One vivid, specific person who represents this audience. Don't stop at what they're saying — get at the psychology underneath it. Give me:*
> - *Who they are: a name plus a 2–3 sentence snapshot of their life, and the moment that brings them to this channel.*
> - *What's really going on beneath the surface.*
> - *What this content does for them — the unmet need it quietly speaks to, and why they keep coming back, in their own words.*
> - *What they're hungry for — the "I wish you would" they're quietly saying — and how to speak to it in the actual words and tones they use, so I can mirror it in titles, hooks, and scripts.*
> - *And what single piece of content this person is quietly asking for that hasn't been made.*

Now we have a specific person. We know exactly who we're making this content for.

### Prompt 3 — find the promise

But what is our **promise** to this specific person? We can hand the avatar to the AI, or write it ourselves — but **where am I taking this person? What is the promise my content has made that is actually going to be the reason they click?**

This is a long prompt, but here's what it asks for:

1. **A promise in the viewer's own words**, so the viewer feels seen and feels like this is for them.
2. **Map the niche** — pull overperforming videos in similar channels and see **what they are already *implicitly* promising**, so we can find **the gap nobody's claiming.**
3. **Draft three candidate promises** — finish the sentence *"Subscribe to my channel because ___."*
4. **Pressure-test it:** *can a hundred other channels say the same thing, or do we really add unique value?*
5. **Implementation** — how to put it in the banner, how to say it in the first seconds of the video, and a **filter to pressure-test that every video fulfills the promise.**

So here we have the promise that was hiding in our own comments, plus a map of what's working for others and what they've been implicitly promising. Then it gives us candidate promises. The one I like best:

> *Subscribe because every video I will make it feel like **you're cooking next to a friend.***

Or:

> *Subscribe because every video will give you a **foolproof recipe that tastes like home.*** (Going back to that nostalgia.)

Honestly I'd tweak it and do something like:

> **"Subscribe to my channel because every video is like cooking with a friend, with foolproof recipes that remind you of home."**

It's calling back to that relationship, calling back to that nostalgia, and **the viewer knows exactly what they're going to get.** Even if you don't go with exactly what you get, it gives you a launching pad to build something stronger, quickly. Then it gives recommendations on the banner, on saying it early in the video, and a filter.
