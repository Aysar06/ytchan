## 1. Premise — the purple cow

**Type:** Alternative context (T06 type 2) — the strongest faceless-compatible type, and the one recommended for saturated niches.

> **Every business, economy and institution is a product. When one breaks, we run the post-mortem.**

Companies, currencies, industries, policies and institutions are analyzed as **software incidents** — severity, timeline, root cause, contributing factors, what shipped, what was ignored, resolution.

**Why this frame and not the others:**
- The SaaS vocabulary is **load-bearing**, not decorative — status pages, uptime, root cause and severity *are* the analysis. Passes the swap test by construction (§N4).
- **Permanently evergreen.** "Why did X collapse" is a 2030 search. Satisfies T01 rule 1.
- **Natively generates the highest-performing title framings.** Failure content *is* validation framing — "I was wrong about X" (4.9× baseline) and "X is dead/dying" (2.1×) are honest descriptions of a post-mortem, not clickbait bolted on (T07/J4).
- **Avoids the losing matchup.** Modern MBA and Company Man run neutral teardowns. We run incident reports. Adjacent to proven demand, not head-on against incumbents (T06/I2).
- **High RPM.** Business/professional audience. Modern MBA earns $5,666/mo from 76 videos.

**The lens test** (must pass for every episode): *"This topic without the incident-report frame is just ___."* If the answer is "the same video, uglier," kill the episode.

## 2. The viewer — built from real comment data

Mined from Modern MBA's comment threads, 2026-07-25 (T05 Prompt 1). **Analytics would have told us "males 25–34." The comments told us something far more useful.**

### What the comments actually revealed

| Signal | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **They bring operational experience** | *"My partner was doing Tranont on the side."* *"I have a family member who was a club owner."* *"Working with Oracle genuinely is extremely disappointing."* *"90% of restaurants fail in the first 2 years. It's location, selection, price, and overhead."* |
| **They watch deep and cite timestamps** | Comments referencing **14:18, 18:39, 35:27** — this audience reaches minute 35 of a 43-minute video and quotes it back. |
| **They fact-check from lived experience** | *"I'm from Alabama, and I definitely remember the Golden Era of Chipotle in 2013."* *"The Nordics love Mexican food, Taco Bell just isn't it."* |
| **They request the next target** | *"herbalife and amway should be in this graveyard."* *"What about WFG?"* |
| **They come to be vindicated** | *"Big money, VCs and the government getting involved actually made things worse?! Nooooooo! No way."* *"They don't call it 'pump and dump' for nothing."* *"Just a scam dressed up in business clothes."* |
| **⚠️ They actively hunt for AI** | *"Was this script written by AI?"* *"Stole the voice right from ewu."* |
| **⚠️ They punish perceived agenda** | *"Paid promotion 😂."* *"This feels like a video made in China."* *"Don't shill for china."* |

### ⭐ Correction to the stated audience
Modern MBA's own description claims *"executives, founders, and working professionals in New York, London, Singapore."* **The comments do not read that way.** They read as people who have *worked inside* businesses — retail, restaurants, an MLM-adjacent family member, an Oracle contract. This is T05's exact point: the claimed audience and the real audience differ, and only the comments tell the truth.

### The avatar

**Dan, 34.** Works in operations or middle management at a company he can see is badly run. He has sat in the meeting where the obviously wrong decision got approved, and he watched it play out exactly as he expected. He puts a 40-minute business video on while doing something else, then stops and rewinds when a number surprises him.

- **The moment he arrives:** something in the news collapsed — a chain, a startup, a currency — and the mainstream coverage feels shallow. He wants the actual mechanism.
- **Underneath:** he isn't cynical, he's **unheard.** He identified the problem at his own job and nobody listened. Watching a well-documented failure is watching someone finally take the analysis seriously.
- **The unmet need:** *proof that he reads situations correctly.*
- **His "I wish you would":** *"Do the one I've been telling people about for years."*
- **His words:** root cause, margins, overhead, unit economics, "they ran it into the ground," "who approved this," receipts.
- **The video nobody has made for him:** a failure post-mortem with the rigor of an engineering incident report instead of a business documentary.

## 3. The promise

> ### Subscribe because every video takes something that failed and runs the post-mortem it never got — with receipts, on the record.

**Pressure test — could 100 other channels say this?** No. Business channels explain *what happened*; this commits to a **fixed forensic method applied to failures only**, with sourcing as part of the format. The commitment to receipts is checkable, which is what makes it a promise rather than a slogan.

**Per-video filter — every episode must answer all four or it doesn't ship:**
1. Did something actually **break**? (No "how X makes money" explainers.)
2. Can we name a **root cause**, not just a story?
3. Is there a **timeline with dates and numbers**?
4. Does the incident-report frame do real work, or is it wallpaper? (§1 lens test)

**Placement:** banner, first 15 seconds of every episode, channel description.

## 5. Title system

Derived from the 137M-video framing data (T07/J4). Failure content generates these honestly.

| Framing | Multiple | Template |
|---|---|---|
| *"I was wrong about X"* | **4.9×** | "I was wrong about [company]" — reserved for genuine reversals only. **Never fake this.** |
| *"X is dead / dying / ruining Y"* | 2.1× | "[Company] is already dead. Here's the timeline." |
| *"X is back / we were right"* | 1.9× | "Everyone called it a scam. They were right." |
| Neutral how-to | 1.0× | **Avoid.** |

**Rules:** lead with the conclusion, never the question. Nuance lives inside the video; the title is a **door for Dan specifically**. Timeless language only — no "in 2026," which stamps an expiry date on the asset (T07/J3).

## 6. Episode template

Runtime **15–25 min** long-form. (Watch time ≫ views: a 27-min video at 43K views produced 5,200 hours and carried a whole channel — T07/J6.)

⚠️ **Episode 001 is the exception: 10–12 min, ~1,600 words.** A 3,000-word first script is 6–9 hours of narration work for a first-time recorder and produces something you hate. Scale to full length once the workflow is automatic. See [narration-guide.md §10](narration-guide.md).

**Script length is arithmetic, not feel:** target minutes × **150 words**. Record at 140–150 WPM; silence-trimming in post lifts effective density to 160–170, which is the calm-but-dense register the reference channels actually run at.

| Beat | Time | Content | Source rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | 0:00–0:20 | The incident card on screen before a word is spoken — severity, duration, one shocking number. One question, one slow push-in, one held beat. | T02 Zone 1 |
| **Small loop closed** | 0:20–0:45 | Give away one real finding immediately. Earn trust, don't hoard the payoff. | T02 / Ryan Tran |
| **The promise** | 0:45–1:00 | State what the post-mortem will establish. | T05 |
| **Mid-action** | 1:00+ | Straight into the failure. **Background and context come later, once they've decided to stay.** | T02 Zone 2 |
| **Timeline** | body | Dated, numbered, dashboard-rendered. New loop **every 60–90s, opened before the last one closes.** | T02 Zone 3 |
| **Root cause** | ~70% | The reveal. Music pause before the line. | T02 |
| **Contributing factors** | | The nuance the title couldn't hold. | T07/J4 |
| **Resolution / status** | close | What actually happened, and the open question that seeds the next episode. | |

**Never:** frontload context, wall-to-wall stimulation, or a neutral title.

## 7. Visual system

Full spec in [vidiq-playbook.md §N](vidiq-playbook.md). Build once as Remotion components, reuse every episode:

`StatusHeader` · `SeverityBadge` · `Timeline` · `RootCauseTree` · `MetricsDashboard` · `TerminalLog` · `Callout`

Slate/neutral canvas, vibrant gradient accents, Inter, rounded corners, glassmorphism, soft glows. **Spring physics on every entrance** — which is also T02's "slow push-in" done natively. Every still gets 15–20% keyframe scale across its duration. Sound-design the graphics: whoosh on slide, tick on highlight, riser into the root-cause reveal, and **cut the music dead before the big line.**

Per Jared Owen ($11,458/mo, 119 videos): a distinctive visual capability *can* be a moat — but only if pushed past generic polish. The component library is the moat; a template anyone can buy is not.

## 8. Risks, named

1. **⚠️ This audience hunts for AI.** Direct evidence in the comments. Mitigations, all mandatory: **narrate in a clone of your own voice** (zero disclosure required, T01/A5); do original research and cite it; and consider Modern MBA's move of stating plainly what is and isn't AI-generated. They turned anti-slop into marketing copy and earn $5,666/mo.
2. **Failure content attracts political heat.** Modern MBA's comments are full of it. Keep the method visible and the ideology absent — the frame is the defense.
3. **Defamation risk.** We are publishing claims about real companies. Stick to documented, sourced facts; attribute contested claims; no invented quotes. This is also T01/A4 — a fingerprint doesn't protect you if you're lying.
4. **Research load is real.** ~15–25 min of sourced analysis per episode. Modern MBA ships 76 videos in 5 years, Micro ships 36. **Low volume, high value is the model — do not try to out-publish the whiteboard wall.**
5. **Topic burnout.** The open question from the start of this project: 50+ videos on business failure. Being watched.
